# Dashboard Design: The Campus Customs Desk

## The vibe I chose

I asked for a dashboard that is lovely and charming, and that feels like a real desk a person would want to spend time at, with creature comforts, a cup of coffee, and a window with sunlight coming through. So the board is a small back office seen from a chair: a painted wall above, an oak desk below, and shop work laid out as things you would find on a real desk.

Agents can be hard to follow when they are shown as a stream of logs. Turning each part of the work into a familiar object makes it clear where to look and what needs a person.

## Layout

| On the desk | What it does | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Corkboard | Holds the three tickets as pinned cards | The whole queue is visible at a glance, and picking a ticket feels like taking a card off the board |
| Open notebook | The left page shows the agents working live, and the right page shows what each agent did | The work and the summary sit side by side |
| Nameplates | Show each agent as resting, working, or finished | I can see who is active without reading the notes |
| In-tray | Holds requests waiting for a signature | Anything that needs a person is in one place, and money never moves by accident |
| Passbook | Shows the checking balance and each payment | The balance drops line by line, like a real bank book |
| Coffee and window | Set the mood | The coffee steams only while the team is working, so even the comforts give a signal |

## The special touch: the light follows the ticket

Each ticket arrived at a real time of day: 9:15 AM, 11:05 AM, and 4:55 PM. When I pick a ticket, the wall clock turns to that time, the sun moves in the window, and the sunlight shifts across the desk. Ticket 103 arrived late in the day, so it brings golden evening light.

The coziness comes from the shop's own data, and each ticket feels like its own moment in the day.

## How agents read differently

Each agent has a color and its own style of writing, so I can tell who is talking without reading the name.

| Agent | Color | Style | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boss | Navy | Clean printed text | Reads like the person in charge |
| Inventory | Brown | Typewriter text on graph paper | Reads like a stock count |
| Accounting | Green | Text on pale green ledger paper | Matches the passbook |
| Facilities | Slate blue | Blue ink | Calm and official, like a lease file |
| Customer Service | Rose | Handwriting | Reads like a personal note to a customer |

Tool use shows up in small italics in plain language, such as "Counted the stock (CC-TEE-WHITE, S)." Handoffs are shaded so I can follow who passed work to whom.

## How resolved tickets show up

A resolved ticket gets a red rubber stamp on its card, which lands with a small thump. A stamp is a clear signal and a small reward for finishing. Until then, each card says where it stands in handwriting: not started, the team is on it, needs your signature, or ready to go back to the team.

A run often ends with a request waiting for a person. Once I sign or decline it, I send the ticket back to the team so the agents can do any work that is now unblocked and close it. I can add a "Note to the team" when I send it back, so a person can give the agents a clear decision.

## How cash shows up

The balance is the largest number in the passbook, because cash is the shop's tightest limit. After I approve a payment, the balance drops and a new line shows the payee, the amount in red, and the new balance. If the requests waiting for approval would be more than the cash on hand, the passbook says how much the shop would be short.

## Keeping a person in control

The agents can only prepare requests. Each request sits in the in-tray and needs a name in the "Signing as" field before its buttons work. The button names the exact amount, such as "Approve and pay $840.00," so I know what I am agreeing to. If the rules refuse a payment, the reason appears on the desk.

## Other choices

- **Fonts:** Typewriter for tickets and bank entries, a warm book serif for reading, and handwriting for notes.
- **Colors:** A dusty blue-green wall and honey oak desk, with Yale navy as the main ink.
- **Accessibility:** The desk works with a keyboard, reads on phone-sized screens, and turns off its motion for people who ask their computer for less motion.
